The brief

Oddman is a clothing line where every piece is sold on pre-order and shipped worldwide. That means the shop is selling clothes that don’t exist yet, so it has to make people confident enough to pay for them up front.

The label needed four things from one product:

A shop with as much attitude as the clothes. Lookbook photography, tilted “tape” navigation, and product pages that read like a magazine spread rather than a catalogue.
Fit confidence without a fitting room. A size guide that sits right beside the size picker, shows only that piece’s sizes, and switches between cm and inches.
A checkout built for its customers. Naira payments through Paystack, and real shipping prices for local and international orders.
An admin simple enough to run solo. Add a new drop, track stock, and see which orders need attention first.

I designed and built all of it: the visual system both sides share, the storefront and the admin.

Outcome

Before, every order was kept as a chat thread, payment alert, or a note on someone’s phone, which was a hassle. Now, the label has one place to access and update every order as it moves from pre-order to production to shipped, easily.

Notifications like new orders, refund requests, and customer complaints all arrive by email as they happen, so nothing goes unnoticed.
